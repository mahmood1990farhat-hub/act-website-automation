# ACT Customer Booking Terms & Conditions

**REVIEW DRAFT v0.1 | 22 October 2026 | NOT APPROVED, PUBLISHED OR EFFECTIVE**

Prepared for Airport & City Transfer (ACT), trading name of Farhat Trading Solutions Ltd. This is a proposed revision of ACT's existing passenger booking terms, not a replacement for the driver-application policy or a driver services agreement. It requires owner and appropriate UK legal review before publication.

## Part A - Proposed customer-facing terms

### About these terms and the contracting party - proposed addition

These terms govern passenger bookings accepted by Farhat Trading Solutions Ltd, trading as Airport & City Transfer (ACT, we, us). Contact us at **info@airportandcitytransfer.com** or **+44 20 8153 0303**. Our verified company, correspondence and private-hire operator details will be shown alongside these terms before publication.

For a London private-hire booking accepted by ACT, ACT contracts as principal with the person making the booking to provide the journey. The passenger's contract is not solely with the individual driver. ACT may use self-employed licensed drivers, but that arrangement does not remove ACT's contractual responsibilities to the customer. Any lawful subcontracting must preserve those responsibilities. [L1]

### 1. Bookings and Confirmations

Bookings must be made through an ACT booking channel that is actually available for the service, such as the website, telephone or an authorised partner. A quotation is not, by itself, a confirmed booking. ACT's acceptance of a booking creates the contract; the booking confirmation records that acceptance and the agreed journey details. Check the pickup and destination, date and local time, passenger numbers, luggage, contact details and any flight or special requirements, and report errors promptly.

Online bookings require at least three hours' notice before pickup. For a shorter-notice request, contact ACT directly; availability and acceptance must be confirmed. This booking lead-time rule is separate from the cancellation rule below. A payment receipt does not substitute for the ACT booking confirmation. If payment appears to have succeeded but you have no confirmation, contact ACT before paying or booking again.

### 2. Payments and Charges

All fares are quoted in GBP (£). Payment is due at booking unless ACT has expressly agreed another arrangement. The quoted total must include applicable taxes and unavoidable charges for the specified journey, including any mandatory booking or airport charge. Where a price cannot reasonably be calculated in advance, ACT must explain how it is calculated clearly and prominently. [L2]

Extra stops, additional waiting or other requested changes may affect the price. Their basis and any additional amount must be disclosed and agreed before the extra service is accepted. ACT will not rely on a general statement that 'additional fees may apply' to introduce an undisclosed mandatory charge. The accepted price will not be increased simply because a different driver is allocated.

### 3. Cancellation at least 3 hours before pickup

If ACT receives your cancellation at least 3 hours before the confirmed pickup time, including exactly 3 hours beforehand, you receive a full refund.

### 4. Cancellation within 3 hours

ACT may deduct reasonable, evidenced losses directly caused by your cancellation, up to the booking price. We take reasonable steps to reduce these losses and account for costs saved or replacement work. Any remaining balance is refunded.

### 5. Passenger no-show

A no-show is assessed only after the agreed waiting period and documented attempts to contact the passenger. Any deduction follows the same loss-based rule as a cancellation within 3 hours and is capped at the booking price.

### 6. Waiting periods

The waiting period is 15 minutes for ordinary pickups and 60 minutes for airport pickups, measured from the agreed pickup time. Any charge for additional waiting must be disclosed and agreed.

### 7. If ACT cannot provide the journey

We offer an alternative for you to accept or a full refund for the journey that cannot be provided.

### 8. Flight delays or cancellations

Contact ACT with any change to your flight. We review and agree revised pickup arrangements. A known flight delay is not automatically treated as a passenger no-show.

### 9. Booking amendments

Changes are subject to availability. Any revised price is disclosed before you accept the change.

### 10. Refund processing

We initiate refunds to the original payment method within 5 working days of confirming the refund amount. Your bank or payment provider may take longer to credit the funds.

### 11. How to cancel or amend a booking

Email info@airportandcitytransfer.com with your booking reference. For urgent changes, also telephone +44 208 153 0303. Cancellation notice is measured from when ACT receives it, using the confirmed pickup time, not when staff reply.

### 12. Use of TfL-Licensed Personnel

Journeys are carried out by TfL-licensed drivers and vehicles. ACT is responsible for allocating drivers and vehicles that meet the applicable licensing, insurance and eligibility requirements. A self-employed arrangement does not reduce those requirements or the customer's contractual protection.

Where ACT lawfully arranges another operator to fulfil a booking, ACT remains responsible to the customer for the journey it accepted. The actual subcontracting model must comply with the relevant licensing rules; ACT will not use these terms to transfer responsibility to an unidentified third party. The appropriate driver and vehicle identification must be supplied before the journey in accordance with applicable requirements.

### 13. Safety and Conduct

Passengers must behave respectfully and follow lawful safety instructions, including applicable seatbelt requirements. Threats, violence, unlawful activity or conduct creating a serious safety risk may justify refusing or stopping a journey. Any decision must be proportionate and must not discriminate unlawfully. The driver will stop at a safe location where practicable and contact ACT where assistance is needed.

ACT will investigate any claim for passenger-caused damage or exceptional cleaning, explain the evidence and calculation, and seek only reasonable, substantiated losses for which the passenger is legally responsible. This clause does not impose an automatic penalty or authorise an unagreed card charge.

### 14. Lost Property

Please contact ACT promptly with the booking reference and a description of any item left in a vehicle. Our existing policy is to log found items and hold them for up to six weeks, subject to safe handling, any legal requirement and the nature of the item. ACT may request reasonable proof of ownership before returning it. Any return-delivery charge must be explained and agreed.

Unclaimed items will be handled through ACT's lawful lost-property procedure; sensitive documents, dangerous items and personal-data-bearing devices require appropriate handling rather than indiscriminate donation. A storage period does not remove any legal claim where ACT is responsible for a loss.

### 15. Complaints Procedure

Complaints may be submitted through the website, by telephone or by email. Give the booking reference and enough information for ACT to investigate, without sending unnecessary sensitive information. ACT will investigate and respond within five working days. If further investigation is needed, ACT should explain the position and give the next update point.

Relevant licensing or safety concerns may also be raised with TfL or the appropriate authority. Using ACT's complaint process does not prevent a customer from exercising statutory rights or approaching a court. ACT will provide any dispute-resolution information it is legally required to supply; no membership of an alternative-dispute-resolution scheme is claimed in this draft.

### 16. Liability

ACT must provide the agreed service with reasonable care and skill and is responsible for foreseeable loss or damage caused by its breach of contract or negligence. A loss is foreseeable if it was an obvious consequence or if, when the contract was made, both parties knew it might result. [L3, L4]

Traffic, weather and other circumstances can affect a journey. Their occurrence does not automatically excuse ACT's own failure to use reasonable care and skill, plan the agreed service appropriately or communicate about a material disruption. Where ACT is not at fault, it is not responsible for losses that it did not cause. Sections 7-10 explain the arrangements for alternatives, amendments and refunds; they do not make a refund the customer's only possible remedy.

Nothing in these terms excludes or restricts liability for death or personal injury caused by negligence, fraud or fraudulent misrepresentation, or liability and consumer rights that cannot lawfully be excluded. There is no general cap limiting every customer claim to the fare paid. [L3, L4, L7]

### 17. Data Protection

ACT processes information needed to manage bookings and journeys, communicate with customers and drivers, handle complaints and meet applicable legal obligations. The separate customer Privacy Notice explains the relevant data, purposes, lawful bases, recipients, retention and rights. Acceptance of these booking terms is not blanket consent to unrelated processing or marketing.

The driver-application privacy notice concerns a different process and does not replace the customer notice. ACT must keep the published notices consistent with its actual suppliers and practices rather than make an unsupported blanket compliance claim.

### 18. Governing Law

These terms are governed by the law of England and Wales, subject to any mandatory consumer protection that applies. They do not remove rights concerning the courts in which a consumer may bring a claim. Different business-account arrangements require separately agreed terms and must not silently replace the protections applying to consumer bookings.

### 19. Passengers, luggage and the booked vehicle - proposed addition

Provide accurate passenger and luggage requirements when booking, including large or unusual items. The vehicle must have sufficient licensed seating and safe luggage capacity; luggage must not obstruct exits or compromise safety. ACT should clarify uncertain capacity before accepting the booking rather than assume an unsafe load can be carried.

A vehicle class is not a promise of a specific make or model unless expressly confirmed. An equivalent or higher-category vehicle may be offered without extra charge where it meets the confirmed requirements. A material downgrade, reduced capacity or unavailable agreed feature requires the customer's agreement to a suitable alternative or the appropriate refund. ACT will not alter an existing confirmed booking unilaterally in a way that removes the customer's agreed protection.

### 20. Accessibility, assistance dogs, pets and child seats - proposed addition

Tell ACT about assistance, mobility-aid, wheelchair-space or child-seat requirements so suitable arrangements can be discussed and confirmed. Early notice helps planning; it does not remove statutory rights. ACT will comply with the applicable duties to assist disabled passengers, provide reasonable adjustments and carry assistance dogs without an unlawful surcharge. The ordinary 'accepts pets' preference does not determine whether an assistance dog can travel. Any valid driver exemption must be handled lawfully by ACT. [L5]

Ordinary pets and any agreed child-seat provision must be confirmed for the booked journey. ACT must not promise that every vehicle has a particular seat or accessibility feature unless it does. The applicable child-passenger safety rules must be followed; this clause does not invent a blanket exemption or guarantee availability.

### 21. Terms acceptance and later changes - proposed addition

The applicable terms must be available before a customer commits to the booking, with important price and cancellation terms clearly presented. ACT should provide the accepted version or an accessible durable copy with the confirmation. Changes to future terms do not retrospectively change an existing booking unless lawfully agreed.

**Proposed booking acknowledgement:** I have checked my journey details, the total price and the cancellation conditions, and I agree to ACT's Customer Booking Terms & Conditions. I acknowledge the separate customer Privacy Notice. This declaration must not be used as consent to unrelated marketing or as a waiver of legal rights.

## Part B - Owner and implementation review, not for publication

### Keep the three documents distinct

**Customer Booking Terms & Conditions:** contract with the person booking an ACT journey. These are the subject of this draft.

**Driver Application Terms and driver Privacy Notice:** application, evidence, verification and portal access. The existing v0.2 draft, including the intended self-employed model and drivers' applicable Self Assessment/HMRC responsibilities, remains unchanged.

**Driver Services Agreement:** the actual working arrangement if a driver is engaged. Application terms alone do not establish commission, payment timing, deductions, working arrangements, substitution, termination or employment status. No commercial terms for that agreement have been invented here. Self-employment must reflect lawful actual arrangements, not only a label, and does not turn ACT into a passenger-booking agent. [L1, L6]

### Specific issues found in the current source

1. **Contracting party:** the terms title uses the brand; the company identity is elsewhere in the footer. Add the legal contracting identity and direct responsibility for accepted journeys. Current registered company/operator details require verification; a public directory is not proof of an operator licence.
2. **Pricing:** the existing open-ended reference to additional charges needs transparent upfront totals and an agreed calculation for extras. Preserve the actual approved fare system; do not put unverified fixed fees in the policy. [L2]
3. **Liability:** the existing text excludes traffic/weather delay liability broadly and limits liability to the fare. Replace it substantively rather than merely adding 'statutory rights unaffected' after the same broad exclusion. Appropriate legal review remains necessary. [L3, L4]
4. **Operational promises:** the source claims exclusively TfL-licensed personnel, six-week lost-property handling, a Compliance Officer response within five working days and full GDPR compliance. Those are source statements, not independently verified operational facts. Check the actual arrangements, especially any subcontracting licence scope, and align them before publication rather than silently relaxing existing customer promises.
5. **Missing customer explanations:** add clear capacity/substitution, accessibility/assistance-dog and version-acceptance wording. Test any related controls separately if changed; a written clause does not implement backend logic. [L5]

### Four finite release checks

**1. Content and identity.** Owner reviews the proposed additions; legal review covers liability, operator responsibility and any business-account variants. Confirm the registered company number, company/correspondence details, licensed operator identity and licence details. The website currently shows a Bath Road address, but this session did not independently verify Companies House/operator records. Do not publish invented licence details or assert this draft is legally certified.

**2. Operational consistency.** Check the acceptance/confirmation event, complete quoted price, amendments, refund handling and operator substitution against the actual website/backend. Preserve cancellation at exactly three hours. A need for manual late-cancellation review is not an automatic no-refund entitlement. A hidden or disabled self-service button must not defeat the published email/telephone cancellation route. Verify existing waiting, complaints and lost-property commitments without rerunning unrelated tests or creating live bookings.

**3. Documents and language.** After wording approval, update the appropriate locale source, downloadable passenger terms PDF, hash manifest, checkout link and confirmation reference together. Reuse `scripts/build-customer-documents.py`; do not overwrite passenger terms with driver terms. Keep the current 18-heading lineage and a reviewable version/date. The English review is not a claim that Arabic or other translations have been reviewed. The existing passenger Privacy Notice remains a separate unresolved review item; the new driver notice is not its replacement.

**4. Acceptance and approval.** Perform only checks appropriate to changed content and interfaces, preserving the already passed UI and build evidence for `98fc471`. That evidence does not certify a future modified revision. Obtain separate exact-revision publication/deployment approval. Keep original sources, PRs, policies, applications and recovery artifacts. Do not create another real application, payment, notification replay or migration for this document review.

**External research, separate from ACT source facts**

- **L1 - TfL Notice 06/22, operator contracts with passengers.** London private-hire operators must contract as principal for the accepted journey. The notice remains linked from TfL's notices index; page 1 was rendered and inspected. [Official notice](https://tfl.gov.uk/cdn/static/cms/documents/tph-notice-06-22-private-hire-operators-contracts-with-passengers.pdf).
- **L2 - CMA, clear and accurate prices.** Official guidance updated 7 January 2026 addresses complete upfront totals and mandatory charges. [Price-transparency guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/price-transparency-cma209/providing-clear-and-accurate-information-about-prices-summary).
- **L3 - Consumer Rights Act 2015, section 57.** Limits on excluding or restricting statutory service obligations; a fare-only cap is not automatically validated by this provision. [Legislation](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/57/2023-01-08).
- **L4 - GOV.UK/CMA, unfair contract terms.** Review broad exclusions, unilateral changes, disproportionate cancellation charges and liability for others used to perform the service. [Writing fair contracts](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/writing-a-fair-contract-for-customers); [Unfair consumer contracts](https://www.gov.uk/unfair-terms-in-sales-contracts/unfair-consumer-contracts).
- **L5 - Department for Transport, disabled passengers.** Duties include reasonable mobility assistance and assistance-dog carriage, with applicable exemptions and restrictions on additional charges. [Rights of disabled passengers](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/rights-of-disabled-passengers-on-transport).
- **L6 - GOV.UK, employment status.** Self-employed tax status and employment-rights status can differ; actual arrangements matter. [Self-employed and contractor status](https://www.gov.uk/employment-status/selfemployed-contractor).
- **L7 - Consumer Rights Act 2015, section 65.** Restricts exclusion of death or personal-injury liability arising from negligence. [Legislation](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/65).

These sources inform proposed drafting, not a legal certification of ACT's business. No legal claim is made that every required notice, licence condition or operational control has been exhaustively checked.
